# Hiring Manager Interview Preparation

This document contains key preparation questions, structured answers, and strategic frameworks for hiring manager interviews, with a specific focus on alignment with company mission, technology challenges, business growth, and feature prioritization.

---

## 1. Why do you want to work at thermondo?

> **Key Alignment:** Connects the mission, technology challenges, and business opportunity.

There are three main reasons why I would like to join thermondo.

**First, the mission.** I really connect with your goal of making sustainable energy accessible to homeowners. Especially with rising energy costs, I think this is becoming increasingly important. What impressed me is that you don't just promote heat pumps, but also try to understand customers' concerns, such as installation costs and electricity prices, and address them through solutions like thermondo Flex, which makes the investment more accessible through financing.

**Second, the technology.** I'm excited about your direction towards AI and digitalisation. I noticed initiatives like the ROPIN app, which supports installation processes, feedback and documentation. I think this is a great example of using technology to solve real operational problems and support your overall mission.

**Third, the business growth.** I'm impressed by how thermondo has expanded its business, reaching around €212 million in revenue in 2025, with a broader portfolio covering heat pumps, photovoltaics and battery storage. I'm also interested in your expansion into multi-family buildings, because this creates new challenges for mobile applications, with more stakeholders, more complex workflows and new integration requirements.

Overall, I see an opportunity to contribute to a meaningful mission while working on challenging technology that directly supports the company's growth.

## 2. How would you prioritise a feature that improves the customer experience but increases engineering complexity?

> **Key Alignment:** Evaluates business value, operational costs, technical risks, and measurable outcomes.

I would start by understanding the problem we are trying to solve and how much value the feature would bring to customers and the business.

For example, at thermondo, a feature that makes it easier for customers to track their heat pump installation could improve transparency and reduce support requests. That creates value not only for customers but also for the operations team.

Then I would evaluate the engineering complexity, including implementation effort, maintenance costs, dependencies and technical risks. I would also consider whether the feature could affect the reliability of existing workflows.

If the value is high but the complexity is also high, I would look for a smaller first version that delivers the most important benefit. We could release it to a limited group, measure results such as customer satisfaction, support requests and task completion rates, and then decide whether to expand it.

My goal is not to avoid complexity, but to make sure the complexity is justified by measurable business and customer value.
